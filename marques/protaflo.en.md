# Protaflo (prostatediscovery.com) — brand file, 25 October 2026

Read: 32 ads (full text) from the Protaflo page, 22 videos, 10 hooks transcribed. New advertiser that appeared on 15 October 2026.

## In one sentence
Protaflo sells a prostate supplement through a **long video sales letter** (VSL, 3 to 5 minutes) saying that saw palmetto, coffee and surgery don't work, and that the real problem is a **"microbe" inside the prostate** that must be cleaned out. 300 ads on 23 October, 350 on 25 October (+17% in 2 days).

## Marketing angles (hooks quoted)
- **The rapid-fire denial** (hook #1, 6 videos out of 10): "Best way to shrink a swollen prostate? Cutting out coffee? Nope. Saw palmetto? Nope. Surgery? Definitely not. Here's what actually works." Three false solutions swept away in 5 seconds, then the promise.
- **The reversal**: "Alcohol is bad for your prostate, right? Wrong. Here's how to shrink your prostate fast." A common belief flipped to stop the thumb.
- **The wife who shares**: "People keep sending me this video saying it has the fastest way to shrink your husband's swollen prostate. Let's see." (their best-ranked ad, 14 variants).
- **The secret the doctor hides**: "I'm going to show you something about your prostate that no doctor ever explains. And it's the real reason nothing you've tried has worked."
- **The symptom list** (text of every ad): "A weak stream… Constant nighttime trips… The inability to urinate… Embarrassing dribbles… An enlarged prostate… And even intimacy issues… Are all symptoms of a much bigger PROSTATE problem."

## Target
- Men 50+ who get up "3, 4, 5 times a night", can't empty their bladder, and have already tried saw palmetto.
- Who tells the story: a man (male voice-over, "we're being lied to" tone), plus a variant where **the wife** shares the video ("your husband's swollen prostate").
- Awareness level: **problem-aware and disappointed by the solutions** (he knows saw palmetto and Flomax, he's looking for something else). Exactly the customer Uromend targets, but Protaflo tells him Uromend's formula "doesn't work".

## Promises
- "Shrink your prostate fast", "do THIS twice a day to shrink prostate naturally" (sales page headline).
- A 3-step method: 1️⃣ "Cleanse harmful microbes" 2️⃣ "Reduce inflammation and swelling" 3️⃣ "Restore balance to the prostate microbiome".
- Announced results: "Stronger urinary flow • Fewer nighttime bathroom trips • Better sleep and energy • Better stamina in the bedroom".
- **90-day money-back guarantee**. "Doctor-approved method", no name given.
- Magic word: the **prostate microbiome** (= the bacteria living in the prostate). Real in science, but here it is the "hidden enemy" that explains why "nothing has worked".

## What it means for Uromend
- **A head-on competitor**, on the same pain (night trips, weak stream) and the same audience, and it **attacks your formula by name** ("Saw palmetto? Nope."), like STĒLO. Two brands now tell the customer your ingredients don't work: this needs an answer, not silence. Idea: an ad "Saw palmetto doesn't work? It depends on the dose" with Uromend's 1,400 mg and the full label (your "honest doses" angle), against the usual 320 mg.
- **The 4-5 minute VSL format in the feed** works well enough to go from 300 to 350 ads in 2 days: a 60-year-old man watches a long video if the hook is good. Uromend has no video over 60 seconds.
- **The "Nope / Nope / Definitely not" hook** can be copied as is with your own enemies: "Flomax? Nope. Surgery? Nope. 320 mg of saw palmetto? Not enough."
- **The "wife who shares" variant** (14 variants = their best ad) confirms what we see at Elivora and Everly: the wife talking about her husband is the narrator who sells. Uromend still has no ad carried by a wife.
- Watch at the next count: above 500 ads = the VSL is profitable, they will flood "Flomax" and "enlarged prostate".
