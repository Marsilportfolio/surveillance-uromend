## 2. Size estimate (monthly revenue and MRR)
Two independent methods, then cross-checked.

**Method A: from traffic.**
- Similarweb shows "< 100K": for a site with this global rank (≈ 1.15M) and +52% growth, the realistic range is **30,000 to 80,000 visits/month**.
- Visit → order conversion for a DTC supplement sold via advertorial: 1.5% to 3% (benchmark 1.6% all categories, Triple Whale 2025; 2 to 3.5% "better-for-you CPG").
- Average order value: 1 bottle $49-60, 3 bottles $110-150; with the 3-bottle "most popular", **AOV ≈ $85 to $100** (all-category benchmark $72).
- Result: 30,000 × 1.5% × $85 = **$38,000** (low); 80,000 × 3% × $100 = **$240,000** (high). Median ≈ 55,000 visits × 2.2% × $90 ≈ **$110,000/month**.

**Method B: from ads.**
- 200 active ads on one page, 5 winners for 5 months, paid influencers: the profile of an advertiser spending **$60,000-150,000 per month on Meta** (brands with 100-300 active ads in this niche typically run in that range; Everly with 9,300 ads is in another league).
- ROAS (revenue / ad spend) of a young DTC supplement: 1.5 to 2.5 (agency median 2.96 on mature brands, 1.86 all categories).
- Result: 60,000 × 1.5 = **$90,000**; 150,000 × 2.5 = **$375,000**. Median ≈ 100,000 × 2 = **$200,000/month**.

**Cross-check**: the two methods overlap between **$90,000 and $240,000 of monthly revenue**. Central estimate: **≈ $150,000/month**, i.e. an annual run rate of **≈ $1.8M** (range $1.1M to $2.9M).

**MRR (the recurring part, subscriptions)**: at 13 months old, the subscription share of a supplement brand is 15 to 30% of revenue (30-45% only at maturity, per Eightx), with 45-55% of subscribers lost within 6 months. **Estimated MRR: $20,000 to $60,000/month**, center ≈ $35,000. The rest (≈ $115,000) is one-time purchases bought with ads: if ads stop, revenue falls by a third within a month.

**Probable profitability**: with 25-35% of revenue in ads (the norm under $1M of revenue) and a $60 product costing $6 to $10 to make, gross margin is strong but net margin is thin or negative in the acquisition phase. The "50% off 24h" and "we messed up" signals are classic cash-flow relaunch ads.

**Verdict: a small, fast-growing DTC brand**, not a big one. For reference: under $5M/year = small; $5-20M = mid-size; Everly (9,300 ads) is probably > $20M. Uromend (133 ads) is below STĒLO in ad volume, so STĒLO is the competitor "just above", the one we can catch.

## 3. The three markets
1. **Prostate supplements**: ≈ $4.2B worldwide in 2025, +6.3%/year to $6.9B in 2034 (Marketintelo); US urology supplements ≈ $1.6B in 2035 at +6.5%/year (GM Insights). Steady growth driven by aging: a mature market, saturated with saw palmetto, where you win with story and proof, not with the ingredient.
2. **Equol**: tiny. Equol supplements ≈ $5 to 9M worldwide (Global Info Research $5.5M in 2024 → $8.6M in 2031; Intel Market Research $4.9M → $7.9M in 2032, +6%/year). The "$1.2B" figures going around are reports for sale, to ignore. So STĒLO isn't riding a growing equol market: **it creates the demand itself through ads**, and at $1.8M/year it would already be 20 to 30% of the worldwide equol supplement market. Equol is a marketing argument, not an underlying wave.
3. **Subscription / MRR (health DTC)**: industry norms: subscription 30-45% of revenue at maturity, 45-55% of subscribers lost within 6 months, customer acquisition cost $80-130 (median ≈ $89), target LTV/CAC ≥ 3. STĒLO is below maturity on subscription (product launched 13 months ago, negative reviews about recurring deliveries).

## 5. "Failed solutions" strategy for Uromend
Principle (Eugene Schwartz, sophisticated market): when the customer has already tried several solutions, you no longer sell "a solution", you sell "**the reason the previous ones failed**", then the mechanism that fixes that reason. STĒLO does exactly that against us ("they were never going to work"). Uromend must go one level higher, including STĒLO in the list of failed solutions.

**The list of failed solutions (with the reason, quantified):**
1. **Flomax / tamsulosin**: relaxes the muscle, doesn't shrink the prostate; dizziness when standing up (orthostatic hypotension), retrograde ejaculation; "every refill just relaxed the pipe while the swelling got worse".
2. **Finasteride**: blocks DHT everywhere, lower libido and erection problems; effects sometimes lasting.
3. **Saw palmetto alone**: STEP (NEJM 2006, 225 men) and CAMUS (JAMA 2011, 369 men): no better than placebo. Reason: alone, at 320 mg, without the other actives.
4. **Cheap pumpkin seed oil alone** (Everly, $15): pumpkin seed has a real study (GRANU, 1,431 men, 12 months) but a $15 softgel oil is underdosed in beta-sitosterol; "phytosterols so watered down your body can't use them" (their own ad).
5. **Amazon "proprietary blend" mixes**: hidden doses, "too small to print" (an existing Uromend angle).
6. **Equol (STĒLO)**: a phytoestrogen, one study of 18 men without placebo, "only 23% of men produce it", a $5M market: a niche molecule sold as a revolution.
7. **Black seed oil** (Purely Nutrient): parasite/biofilm angle, no placebo-controlled prostate study.
8. **Nattokinase** (Noverly): a circulation enzyme, repurposed for the prostate.

**The Uromend mechanism that answers each failure**: "6 ingredients, each at the study dose, printed on the label": pumpkin seed 600 mg (GRANU), saw palmetto 250 mg **combined** (not alone), beta-sitosterol 200 mg dosed separately (what $15 pumpkin seed oil lacks), nettle, turmeric, reishi for inflammation; zero estrogen, zero prescription, zero dizziness.

**"Failed solutions" ad formats to produce:**
- **Native black text**: "I tried Flomax, then saw palmetto, then the "Japanese molecule". Here is why each one failed, and what finally worked." (first-person story, 5 blocks, red arrows).
- **List**: "4 prostate "fixes" that failed men over 50, and the one reason they all failed: wrong dose, or the wrong molecule."
- **Table** (like 1.4), extended: Flomax / saw palmetto alone / equol / Uromend MEN, rows "tested against a placebo", "side effects on sex drive", "doses printed", "number of active ingredients".
- **30-second video**: "Every prostate supplement failed you for the same reason" (STĒLO's hook flipped) → "not because of DHT, because of the dose".
- Awareness level targeted: **solution aware, disappointed** (level 3), the same one STĒLO recruits. Enemies: the failed solutions, never a named brand.
