En une phrase

Un petit nouveau (page Facebook « Healthy Living After 55 », site north-hollow.com) qui vend une gélule sèche à 8 plantes, presque la même famille de formule qu'Uromend, en recopiant les meilleures mécaniques des gros : l'urologue repenti d'Elivora, le « STOP taking prostate supplements » d'Everly et le « voyage de la gélule » de Purely Nutrient.

Angles marketing (accroches citées)

  • L'urologue contre le Tamsulosin (le nom générique du Flomax) : « If you are on Tamsulosin and still getting up 2-3 times a night WATCH THIS NOW. Tamsulosin does not heal your prostate. It is your one-way ticket to a catheter and an operation. » Avec un chiffre : « A 2018 European study found that 57 percent of men on Tamsulosin for 5 to 10 years ended up needing surgery anyway. » (voir, 2 variantes, lancée le 11 novembre).
  • Le voyage de la gélule, heure par heure : « Here's what one capsule of North Hollow does inside your prostate, starting the second you swallow it… Hour 2… Hour 12… Hour 24… Week 3 » (voir). Phrase clé : « It's not aging. It's compression. »
  • « Please STOP taking prostate supplements » : la copie mot pour mot de la pub d'Everly (Dr. Anastasia Chamberlen), ici pour vendre une formule complète contre « cheap saw palmetto powder » (voir).
  • La peur de l'opération : « I'm 58 and I had prostate surgery 18 months ago. Three weeks later my wife found me crying in the garage. » (TURP = on retire une partie de la prostate en passant par l'urètre ; voir).
  • L'épouse qui raconte : « My husband started taking Flomax on March 8th, 2024… He still got up five times a night… your prostate is 74 grams » (voir).

Cible (âge, sexe, douleur, qui raconte)

Hommes de 55 à 75 ans déjà sous Tamsulosin / Flomax, qui se lèvent 2 à 5 fois par nuit et à qui l'urologue commence à parler d'opération. Ceux qui racontent : un urologue « de 25 ans de métier », un homme de 58 ans opéré, une épouse. La page s'appelle « Healthy Living After 55 » : l'âge est dans le nom.

Promesses

Moins de levers la nuit, jet plus fort, vessie mieux vidée, sommeil plus profond. « Triple-Action: Hormone Balance | Healthy Blood Flow | Cellular Cleanse ». Garantie 30 jours.

It's always the guy who says "it's just once or twice a night" who ends up on the floor.
My name is Dale. I'm 61. I ran a machine shop for 34 years, and I don't fall for supplement ads. Before I go on, I want to be clear about something.
I knew men my age get up at night. My doctor told me that. Your doctor probably told you too. Every man over 50 in my family made the same trip down the same dark hallway.
Here's what nobody told me: those trips are not "just getting older." They aren't a tax you pay for turning 50.
They're a five-stage warning. Most men don't know which stage they're in until something happens to them in the dark.
Here's what the stages actually look like. Most men only know the phrase "I get up a couple times." They don't know where it goes from there.
STAGE ONE. The extra trip.
Around 50, a gland the size of a walnut starts growing. It's your prostate, and it wraps right around the tube you pee through. As it grows, it squeezes that tube like a kinked garden hose.
At first you don't notice. You get up once around 2 AM, go back to bed, and think nothing of it. You stop drinking after dinner and figure you've solved it.
You haven't. The hose is still getting kinked, a little more every year.
STAGE TWO. The slow start.
You stand at the toilet and wait for it to start. When it comes, it's weak. It stops, then starts again. You shake, zip up, and ten minutes later you feel like you have to go again.
That's because your bladder never actually emptied. There's a little left in there every time. So it fills back up faster, and it wakes you up faster.
Most men live at stage two for years and call it normal. I did.
STAGE THREE. The foam.
This is the one I ignored the longest.
I'd look down and see bubbles in the bowl that didn't go away. Foam sitting on top of the water. Sometimes it was there every morning.
I asked my doctor about it. You know what he said. The same thing everybody's doctor says:
"Drink more water."
Think about that. I was already getting up three times a night, and the fix was to drink MORE?
So I did what he said. And the nights got worse.
(Here's what I wish someone had told me back then: foam that shows up every day is worth a real urine test. It's a simple test, and it tells you whether protein is in there. Ask for it by name. I finally did. Mine came back clean. But I should have asked two years earlier instead of just drinking more water.)
STAGE FOUR. The 3 AM math.

Your eyes are puffy in the mirror. You hit a wall at 2 in the afternoon. You snap at your wife over nothing. You nod off in the recliner at 7:30 PM and then you can't sleep when it's actually time to go to bed.
You tell yourself this is just what 60 feels like.
It isn't.
