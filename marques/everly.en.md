## In one sentence
Everly is an ad factory: **≈ 9,300 active ads on 9 Facebook pages** (3 pages at 1,500 each), one product (pumpkin seed oil softgels, ≈ $15 entry price, 3-bottle pack pushed), and the same story endlessly adapted to **4 different pain points**: prostate/nights, kidneys, women's bladder, hair. Uromend has 133.

## Marketing angles (from 84 collected ads)
- **"Stop the Flomax"** (closest to Uromend): "My husband refused Flomax for four years", "There are 3 stages of prostate failure. Stage 1 is Flomax. Stage 3 is a catheter bag", "Tamsulosin relaxes smooth muscle around a prostate that never stops growing". The drug is presented as a trap that hides the real problem (DHT, the hormone that enlarges the prostate) and leads to the catheter or surgery.
- **Fear of the catheter / surgery**: "My husband wore a catheter bag strapped to his thigh for 19 days", "My husband had prostate surgery 8 weeks ago". The catheter is the recurring horror image, always seen through the wife's eyes.
- **Kidneys & dialysis** (big angle, surprising for pumpkin seed): "Your kidneys contain roughly 1 million tiny filters", "If your pee looks like someone poured dish soap into the toilet… your kidneys are screaming", "37. That was my eGFR". The ads link foamy urine, night trips and dialysis. Transcribed videos confirm: "Flush your kidneys in just one night", "You don't have a weak bladder. You probably have stressed kidneys".
- **Women's bladder** (pads, leaks): "If you're a woman over 45 who's wearing a pad every single day — not for your period, for your BLADDER", "I've been a surgical nurse for 27 years, and bladder leakage is one of those things nobody talks about". Dedicated landing page "tired-of-hair-shedding-sleepless-nights-sudden-urges…".
- **Hair / hormones** (women, levothyroxine, Austrian dermatologist): same product, completely different target.
- **The forgotten scientist**: "In 1985, a German urologist named Dr. Werner Vahlensieck ran a clinical trial… the pharmaceutical industry spent 40 years making sure you never heard about it".
- **Fake confession / "Don't buy"**: "I need to publicly apologize to anyone who's ever bought Everly… Because I lied", "Do not buy Everly Pumpkin Seed Oil, especially if you're a man over 40" (video). An old copywriting trick that forces people to read.
- **Catalog ad** (the most duplicated, 5 variants, since January 2026): "🎃 Looking for natural support for your body? 🌙 bladder 💧 kidneys 💪 hair… Just 2 softgels a day". Their "safe" ad, running for 9 months.

## Target
- **Men over 45-50** who get up 3 to 6 times a night, already on Flomax/tamsulosin/finasteride, afraid of the catheter and of surgery.
- **Their wives**: a huge number of ads are written in the first person by the wife or a nurse ("My husband…"). The woman is the one who buys for him.
- **Men and women over 40 worried about their kidneys** (eGFR, dialysis, foamy urine).
- **Women over 45**: leaks, pads, hair loss, thyroid.

## Promises
- Sleep through the night without getting up; strong stream; empty the bladder; "block the DHT".
- Avoid the catheter, surgery, dialysis (promise through fear).
- Results in "8 weeks" / "12 weeks"; "wake up with real energy".
- Thicker hair, fewer leaks (women).

## Offers and landing pages
- Single product: Everly Pumpkin Seed Oil softgels 3000 mg, cold-pressed, "Made in USA".
- Price hook in short videos: "only $15", "deal expires tonight", 3-bottle pack pushed ("don't make the same mistake I made").
- Landing pages: [try.shopeverly.shop/esl](https://try.shopeverly.shop/esl) (advertorial = fake article) for long stories; the Shopify product page for catalog ads; "5 reasons", "hidden hormone" pages and a women's page "tired-of-hair-shedding…".

## Formats
- **Image + very long copy** (3,000 to 4,000-character story): the dominant format of the "Dr. Lisa Downing", "Nutritionist - Katherine Davis" and "Rooted Remedies" pages. A fake doctor or a nurse tells a case (Gerald, Ray, Frank, Gary…), with medical numbers (eGFR 37, 0.4 mg → 0.8 mg).
- **Short UGC videos** (30 s to 1 min): one person on camera, "click that orange cart", $15 price, urgency.
- **Long videos** (3 to 5 min): voice-over "nephrologists won't tell you", foamy urine.
- **9 "avatar" pages** (invented doctor or nutritionist names, "Rooted Remedies", "Elemental Grove"…): if Meta blocks one page, the others keep running.

## Hooks, awareness level, personas, enemies, narrative (indicative figures from 10/17; the charts below are recalculated at each snapshot)
- **#1 hook: the catalog** (49%: "🎃 Looking for natural support for your body?"): their "safe" ad repeated on every page. Then the symptom question (20%: "Still getting up 3+ times a night?", "If you're a man over 45 and you've noticed foam…"), the wife's story (7%), the shock number (5%: "1 million tiny filters", "37. That was my eGFR"), authority (5%).
- **Awareness level**: mostly **solution aware** (61%: he already knows pumpkin seed oil, they tell him "take it properly / the right dose") and **problem aware** (19%). 12% "most aware" ($15 price videos).
- **Persona**: man 50+ prostate (43%), **kidney patient** (36%: foam, eGFR, dialysis), woman 45+ bladder/hair (12%), wife (5%).
- **Enemy**: the drug (29%), "it's just age" (11%), surgery/catheter/dialysis (7%). A third of the ads have no enemy (catalog).
- **Narrative**: catalog/offer (48%), testimonial (8%), educational list (6%), expert story (6%).

## What it means for Uromend
- Everly doesn't sell pumpkin seed, it sells **one story per pain point**. Uromend has 6 ingredients and a single message ("6-in-1 formula"): the story is missing. A single advertorial "My husband refused Flomax…" generates dozens of variants for them.
- The **catheter / surgery** angle (seen by the wife) is their strongest emotional lever. Uromend can use it honestly: "before it gets to that".
- **Long** ad copy (3,000 chars) works for them: don't be afraid of long text on Facebook, the target is 60 and reads.
- Their weakness: a single ingredient at 3,000 mg and a $15 entry price. Uromend can attack with "pumpkin seed alone isn't enough: you need saw palmetto + beta-sitosterol + nettle, at study doses" (which the Uromend ad "Most prostate brands hide behind a proprietary blend" already does).
- Copying their multi-page system is risky (fake doctors = misleading ads), but a **second legitimate Facebook page** (e.g. "My Bladder And Prostate", already created) with real customer stories is the clean version.
