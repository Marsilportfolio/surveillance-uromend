## In one sentence
Elivora is **Uromend's closest competitor**: pumpkin seed + saw palmetto + beta-sitosterol, "Stop taking Flomax" angle, **≈ 1,340 active ads** on 2 pages (Veda Lockwood 1,000, Christopher Jay 340), almost all launched in **October 2026** (45 of the 60 collected): they are in full acceleration. And since early October the Christopher Jay page has been testing a **second product, erection gummies** (ultimapeak.com).

## Marketing angles (60 ads read)
- **"Stop taking Flomax"** leads: "I'm a urologist, and here are 3 things men over 50 need to stop doing immediately if they keep getting up to pee at night. Number one: Stop taking Flomax" (4th on the "Flomax" search, 18th on "bladder support"). Flomax "relaxes the muscle" but "doesn't shrink what is swollen"; only "the one cold-pressed oil" does.
- **The husband who refused the drug**: "My husband refused Flomax / tamsulosin / finasteride / tadalafil for four years. Here's what happened to his prostate": the same ad exists with 4 different drug names (Oct 7, 2026) to capture each search. Narrator: a nurse of 28 years who "catheterized men who couldn't empty their own bladders".
- **The 3 a.m. fall**: "Flomax had already put him face down on the hallway floor at 3 a.m., bleeding from a split eyebrow" (a real Flomax side effect: orthostatic hypotension, dizziness when standing up). Very concrete angle.
- **Dangerous foods for the prostate**: "Here are the most dangerous foods for your prostate. I do not care if you do not believe me". List format.
- **The beer given up for nothing**: "Too many men who poured their beer down the sink… and still got up three times a night". Speaks to the guy who already made efforts.
- **Founder story**: "My father stopped buying pumpkin seed oil because none of it ever worked. So I flew to Styria to source the real thing" (Styria = a region of Austria, the "reference" pumpkin seed). And the fake confession "Please don't buy Elivora… We didn't warn you what it feels like to sleep through the night again".
- **"We lied"**: "We need to publicly apologize… because we lied. Yes, we're using cold-pressed virgin pumpkin seed oil, not the heat-processed powder that wrecks the fragile beta-sitosterol and delta-7 sterols": they attack powders (so pumpkin-powder capsules like… Uromend).
- **Kidneys / eGFR** (July 2026, inherited from Everly): "I need to tell you something that might piss you off about your nephrologist".
- **New since October: erections** (Christopher Jay page, 19 ads to ultimapeak.com): "What Happens When Your New Wife Wants Your D*ck More Than You Can Give?", "I've been lying to men about their ED for 15 years", "My Husband's Doctor Gave Him Viagra. Six Months Later He Was Dead", a "senior Pfizer researcher" who quits. Very crude language, stories of remarriage at 51 to a younger woman.

## Target
- **Men aged 50 to 70** on Flomax/tamsulosin, 3 to 6 night trips, afraid of the catheter ("thread a tube into themselves three, four, five times a day") and of surgery.
- **Their wives/nurses** as narrators ("My husband…", "I prepped men for prostate surgery for 31 years").
- Since October: **men aged 47 to 58, divorced or remarried** with erection problems, and their wives ("For any woman whose husband is brushing off his erection problems").

## Promises
- Going from 3-6 night trips to 0-1 "in 12 weeks" / "four weeks after… he was getting up once a night".
- "Shrinks what is swollen": a stronger promise than "supports".
- Avoid the catheter, surgery, Flomax dizziness.
- Gummies: erections "without the blue pill".

## Formats
- **Almost 100% image + long copy** (only 1 video out of 60): a "proof" photo (man in bed, bottle, prescription) + a 3,000-character story signed by a fake urologist ("Dr. Veda Lockwood", 23 years of practice) or a nurse.
- The ads are **copied word for word from Everly** ("My husband refused Flomax for four years", "My Husband Was Getting Up 6 Times a Night", "We need to publicly apologize"): same team or same template.
- Variants per keyword (Flomax / tamsulosin / finasteride / tadalafil): one ad per searched drug.

## What the videos say
- Only one video collected (Christopher Jay page, Oct 14, 2026, the "senior Pfizer researcher" story for the gummies): empty transcript, it's a video without voice (on-screen text + music). Elivora is a **100% long copy + image** brand: the whole pitch is in the ads below, readable in full.

## Hooks, awareness level, personas, enemies, narrative (indicative figures from 10/17; the charts below are recalculated at each snapshot)
- **#1 hook: the wife's story** (40% of ads): "My husband refused Flomax / tamsulosin / finasteride / tadalafil for four years". Then authority ("I'm a urologist…", 10%), fake confession ("Please don't buy Elivora", "we lied", 10%), list ("3 things men over 50 need to stop doing", 8%).
- **Awareness level (Schwartz)**: they mostly speak to the **problem aware** (50%: the man knows he gets up at night, that he's on Flomax) and the **unaware** (35%: "here's what your urologist doesn't tell you"). Almost never the "most aware" (price/promo: 2%): Elivora runs no promos in its ads, it educates and scares.
- **Persona**: the man aged 50-70 on medication (63%), then the erection man (15%, new in October), the wife (12%).
- **Enemy**: the **drug** in 95% of ads (Flomax first, tamsulosin = its generic, finasteride, tadalafil, silodosin). Not DHT, not other brands: the enemy is the prescription.
- **Narrative**: the first-person testimonial (40%), then the expert story and the educational list (10% each). Never a catalog.

## What it means for Uromend
- It's **the competitor to copy smartly**: same target, same promise, 10 × the volume. Their best hook ("3 things men over 50 need to stop doing… Number one: Stop taking Flomax") can become a Uromend video with a real face.
- Their attack on **powders** ("heat-processed powder that wrecks the beta-sitosterol") directly targets capsule formulas like Uromend: prepare the answer (full doses, 6 actives, beta-sitosterol dosed separately, not "hidden" in an oil).
- The **one ad per drug** trick (Flomax, tamsulosin, finasteride, tadalafil, and also silodosin) is easy to replicate: Uromend has only one ad "Say goodbye to weak and expensive Rx treatments".
- The **erection** pivot (Christopher Jay page) shows where the money goes: prostate → sexual performance, same customer. Uromend has nettle and saw palmetto in its formula: a "libido/confidence" angle is possible without changing the product.
- To watch at the next count: if Veda Lockwood goes above 1,500 ads, they are scaling the Flomax angle; if ultimapeak grows, they are pivoting.
