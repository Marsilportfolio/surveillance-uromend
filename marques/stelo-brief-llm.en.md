## 4. What the studies say (PubMed, checked 10/17/2026)
- **The only clinical study on equol + prostate**: Lephart, Open Journal of Urology 2013 (SCIRP, pay-to-publish): **18 men, 4 weeks, no placebo group**, 6 mg of equol twice a day. The "87% / 88% / 67%" come from there. No randomized placebo-controlled trial on equol and benign prostatic hyperplasia exists in PubMed.
- **Equol is a phytoestrogen** ("chemical structure similar to estrogen", acts on ERβ receptors): J Diabetes Investig 2023, DOI 10.1111/jdi.13995. The STĒLO page says "hormone-free".
- **Equol producers**: 29% to 48% of adults depending on the definition (Clin Chim Acta 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.cca.2024.117885); swallowing equol doesn't change your gut flora.
- **Their "NIH STEP trial" argument**: STEP (NEJM 2006, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa053085, 225 men, 1 year) and CAMUS (JAMA 2011, DOI 10.1001/jama.2011.1364, 369 men) tested **saw palmetto alone** and did no better than placebo. That's correct. But STEP tested neither pumpkin seed nor beta-sitosterol.
- **Pumpkin seed**: GRANU, Urol Int 2014, DOI 10.1159/000362903, **1,431 men, 12 months, placebo-controlled**, pumpkin seed > placebo (58.5% vs 47.3% responders). BMC Urol 2021, DOI 10.1186/s12894-021-00910-8, 73 men: pumpkin seed oil effective with no side effects, less than tamsulosin.
- **Beta-sitosterol**: "improves urinary symptoms and flow measures" (Arch Ital Urol Androl 2019, DOI 10.4081/aiua.2019.3.139).
