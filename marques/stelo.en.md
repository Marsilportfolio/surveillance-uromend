## What the studies really say (from PubMed)
- **The only clinical study on equol and the prostate** is a pilot trial by Edwin Lephart (Brigham Young University, Utah): **18 men, 4 weeks, no placebo group**, published in 2013 in the "Open Journal of Urology" (SCIRP, a pay-to-publish publisher). The "87% / 88% / 67%" on their page come from there. No randomized placebo-controlled trial on equol and the prostate exists in PubMed (search of 10/17/2026).
- **Equol is a phytoestrogen**: "a chemical structure similar to estrogen", it acts on estrogen receptors ([Journal of Diabetes Investigation 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdi.13995)). Their page says "hormone-free": misleading at best.
- **"Only 23% of American men produce Equol"**: equol production varies hugely between people and by definition (29% to 48% producers in a Japanese study of 466 people, [Clinica Chimica Acta 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cca.2024.117885)): swallowing equol doesn't make you "Japanese".
- **Their "NIH STEP trial" argument is twisted**: STEP ([NEJM 2006](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa053085), 225 men, 1 year) and CAMUS ([JAMA 2011](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2011.1364), 369 men) tested **saw palmetto alone** and found it no better than placebo. True, and that's why Uromend doesn't rely on saw palmetto alone. But STEP never tested pumpkin seed or beta-sitosterol.
- **Pumpkin seed has a real placebo-controlled study**: GRANU, [Urologia Internationalis 2014](https://doi.org/10.1159/000362903), **1,431 men, 12 months**, pumpkin seed better than placebo on the symptom score (58.5% responders vs 47.3%). And a 2021 trial ([BMC Urology](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12894-021-00910-8), 73 men): pumpkin seed oil improves symptoms with **zero side effects**, where tamsulosin causes dizziness and retrograde ejaculation.
- **Beta-sitosterol**: the 2019 review ([Arch Ital Urol Androl](https://doi.org/10.4081/aiua.2019.3.139)) describes it as "able to improve urinary symptoms and flow measures".
- Bonus: in Japanese men seeking fertility treatment, more soy isoflavones in urine = lower sperm count ([Asian J Androl 2026](https://doi.org/10.4103/aja202566)); equol itself wasn't associated, but it's the molecule family they sell.

## What it means for Uromend
- STĒLO builds all its marketing on the back of your formula. The honest counter-attack: **"18 men, 4 weeks, no placebo"** vs **"1,431 men, 12 months, placebo-controlled"** for pumpkin seed. That's an ad to make.
- Their ad structure is good and copyable: hook "X, Y, Z were never going to work" → simple mechanism (DHT 5× more powerful than testosterone) → the solution → the offer. Uromend can flip the same structure: "Equol was never going to work. Why? 18 men. 4 weeks. No placebo."
- Careful with "hormone-free": equol is a phytoestrogen, a counter-attack angle ("you're taking a plant estrogen for your prostate"), to use with care (don't claim danger, only state the molecule's nature).
